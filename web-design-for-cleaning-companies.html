The problem

Domestic-looking sites lose
commercial cleaning contracts

Most cleaning company websites are built for householders. Photographs of a kitchen worktop, a contact form, a price for a three-bedroom house. That is fine if domestic is the business you want.

It is a problem the moment you go after commercial and communal contracts. A building manager comparing three contractors is not asking whether you can clean. They are asking whether you can be trusted with a shared entrance hall, a stairwell and a bin store in an occupied block, with residents complaining if it goes wrong.

Nothing on a domestic-looking site answers that. So you get filtered out before anyone reads a word, and you never hear about it.

Before they shortlist you

What a property manager
actually looks for

Three things decide whether you make the shortlist. Most websites for commercial cleaners show none of them.

01

Recent commercial work

Not a gallery of houses. Evidence that you already clean the kind of building they manage — communal areas, offices, blocks of flats — and that the work is recent.

02

Insurance and accreditations

Public liability, employer’s liability, any trade accreditations you hold, and DBS checks where they apply. A manager needs to see these before they can put you forward, not after.

03

Handling occupied buildings

Working around residents and staff, out-of-hours access, key handling, health and safety, and what happens when something goes wrong. This is the part that separates commercial contractors from domestic cleaners.

Being straight about what a website does here: it wins you the small and mid-sized commercial contracts, and it makes you credible when a manager is deciding who to shortlist. It does not win large contracts. Those go through tenders and procurement frameworks, and no website replaces that process.
