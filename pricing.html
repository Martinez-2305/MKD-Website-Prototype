Pricing

Straightforward pricing.
No quotes-on-request.

Most web design companies won’t tell you what anything costs until you’ve sat through a call. I’d rather you knew now.

Two ways to work with me. Same website, same work — the only difference is how you pay for it.

Every site is built by hand for your business. No templates you’ll recognise from three of your competitors, no page builders, no drag-and-drop. Built by me, personally, from the first conversation to the last.

Delivered once, finished at launch.

What you get every month — £199

This is the part that produces results. A website on its own is a brochure; the monthly work is what puts it in front of people.

  • One new page every month — a service, an area, or a page targeting something your competitors rank for and you don’t. Twelve months in, your site is working twelve times harder than it did at launch.
  • Google Business Profile management — weekly posts, photo updates, and a system for getting reviews in without having to nag anyone
  • Search monitoring — I check what you’re ranking for, what’s moved, and what’s newly worth going after
  • Indexation and health checks — so a page that quietly drops out of Google doesn’t stay dropped for four months
  • A one-page report, every month — what happened, what I did, what’s next. In English. Two minutes to read.
  • Content edits and small changes — new services, price updates, staff changes. Just ask.
  • Hosting, domain renewal, SSL, backups and security — all included, nothing extra to buy, no renewal invoices from anyone else
  • Direct access to me — not a ticket system, not an account manager
Minimum term: 6 months on the Own It route, 24 months on the Spread It route. After that, monthly.

If you don’t want the monthly service

Not everyone needs it. If you win your work through tenders, referrals or repeat customers and you just need a site that makes you look like the serious operator you are, that’s a legitimate position.

In that case you’ll need the Care Plan — £45/month: hosting, domain, SSL, backups, security updates and small edits. Everything that keeps the site online and safe, without the search work.

I don’t host sites for free. If something breaks at 11pm, I want us both clear on whether I’m the person fixing it.

Going after bigger contracts

A website gets you shortlisted. It is not the only thing a buyer looks at before they put you forward, and for larger commercial and communal work it is usually not even the first thing.

If that is where you are heading, I also work on the rest of the picture:

  • LinkedIn optimisation — because a facilities manager or procurement lead will look you up there before they call you, and an empty profile answers none of their questions.
  • A consultation on tender-ready documents — what a buyer expects to receive, what usually gets an application set aside, and what you need in place before you bid.
  • A high-value accreditation checklist — which accreditations actually open doors in your sector, which are worth the cost, and the order to go after them in.
  • A capability statement — the short document a buyer asks for when they want to know, quickly, whether you can handle the contract.
No fixed price on this one, and I would rather say why than pretend otherwise. Every one of these depends on the size of contract you are chasing, what you already have, and which sector you are bidding into. Publishing a number would mean either overcharging most people or quoting for work I have not scoped. Tell me what you are bidding for and I will quote for your situation.

Questions

Why are your prices on the website?

Because you’re going to ask anyway, and making you sit through a call first wastes your time and mine. If the number doesn’t work for you, you’ve lost thirty seconds instead of an hour.

Isn’t £1,999 a lot for a website?

It’s the going rate for a professionally built one. A regional agency will typically quote £3,000–£6,000 for the same scope, and the £500 sites you’ll find advertised are templates with your logo on them. If your budget is genuinely lower than this, I’m not the right person, and I’ll tell you that on the first call rather than after you’ve paid a deposit.

What happens after the 24 months on the Spread It route?

The site and domain transfer to you, and the £199 continues monthly with no minimum term. Stop whenever you like — 30 days’ notice.

What if I need to cancel the Spread It route early?

The unpaid portion of the build becomes payable, worked out as £1,500 × (months remaining ÷ 24). Cancel at month 12 and that’s £750. It isn’t a penalty — it’s the part of the website you haven’t paid for yet.

Who owns the site?

You do, from launch on the Own It route, and at the end of the term on the Spread It route. Either way, the code is yours — I don’t lock anyone into a platform they can’t leave.

Do I have to take the monthly service?

No, but you do need the £45 Care Plan to keep the site hosted and maintained. The honest version: the build gets you a site, the monthly work is what gets you found. If you only ever do one of them, most businesses in your position should do both.

How long does it take?

Two to four weeks from the point I have your content. The slowest part is usually waiting on photos.

Do you work outside Surrey?

Yes. Most of my work is done remotely and I have clients across the UK. If you’d rather meet, and you’re within reach, I’m happy to.

Are there VAT or hidden costs?

No VAT, and no hidden costs. The price you see is the price, and hosting and your domain are included in the monthly.
